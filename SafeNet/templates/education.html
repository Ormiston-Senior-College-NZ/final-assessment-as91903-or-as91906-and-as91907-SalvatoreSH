What is Phishing?

A scam where criminals pretend to be a trusted organisation to steal your information.

Phishing attacks typically arrive as emails, text messages, or fake websites that look like they are from a bank, delivery company, or government agency. Their goal is to trick you into entering your login credentials, credit card details, or personal information.

warning Warning Signs

  • • Urgent language — "Your account will be suspended!"
  • • Unexpected requests for passwords or card numbers
  • • Sender email doesn't match the company's real domain
  • • Links that hover to a suspicious or misspelled URL
  • • Poor spelling or grammar in the message

shield How to Protect Yourself

  • ✓ Never click links in unexpected emails — go to the website directly
  • ✓ Check the sender's actual email address carefully
  • ✓ Use SafeNet to scan any link before clicking
  • ✓ Enable two-factor authentication on important accounts
  • ✓ Report phishing emails to your email provider

example Real Example

A common NZ phishing scam involves a fake NZ Post email claiming your parcel is on hold. The email contains a link like http://nzpost.delivery-confirm.ru/track — notice it is not the real nzpost.co.nz domain. Clicking it leads to a page that steals your credit card details.

What is Malware?

Malicious software designed to damage, spy on, or take control of your device without your knowledge.

Malware includes viruses, spyware, ransomware, and trojans. It can arrive through email attachments, fake software downloads, infected USB drives, or malicious websites. Once installed, it can steal your data, encrypt your files, or use your device to attack others.

Trojan

Disguises itself as legitimate software to gain access to your system.

shield Protection Tips

  • ✓ Keep your operating system and apps updated
  • ✓ Only download software from official sources
  • ✓ Never open unexpected email attachments
  • ✓ Use reputable antivirus software
  • ✓ Back up your important files regularly
  • ✓ Be cautious of "free" software from unknown sites

Online Scams

Fraudulent schemes designed to trick you into giving away money or personal information.

Online scams come in many forms. They often exploit trust, urgency, or curiosity. Common scams in New Zealand include fake prize notifications, investment fraud, romance scams, and impersonation of government agencies like IRD or ACC.

🚨 Common Scam Types

  • Prize scams — "You've won! Click to claim."
  • Investment fraud — Fake crypto or trading platforms
  • Romance scams — Building fake relationships to request money
  • Tech support scams — "Your computer has a virus, call us now"
  • Government impersonation — Fake IRD or ACC messages

✅ How to Spot a Scam

  • If it sounds too good to be true, it probably is
  • Legitimate organisations never ask for gift card payments
  • Real IRD/ACC will not threaten immediate arrest
  • Always verify by calling the organisation directly on their official number
  • Never transfer money under pressure — scammers create urgency on purpose

Password Safety

Strong, unique passwords are your first line of defence.

✗

Weak Password

password123

Dictionary word + simple numbers. Cracked in seconds.

~

Moderate Password

S@feNet2024

Better, but based on a recognisable word. Could be improved.

✓

Strong Password

k#9Lp!mXqR2$vT

Random mix of characters. Use a password manager to store it.

Pro tip: Use a passphrase — a sentence like Purple-Kiwi-Runs-Fast-42! is both memorable and very secure. Never reuse passwords across sites and enable two-factor authentication wherever possible.
