What is Phishing?

Criminals impersonating trusted organisations to steal your information.

mail

Phishing attacks arrive as emails, text messages, or fake websites mimicking a bank, delivery company, or government agency. Their goal is to trick you into entering your login credentials, credit card details, or personal information.

warning Warning Signs

  • • Urgent language — "Your account will be suspended!"
  • • Unexpected requests for passwords or card numbers
  • • Sender email doesn't match the company's real domain
  • • Links hover to a suspicious or misspelled URL
  • • Poor spelling or grammar in the message

shield How to Protect Yourself

  • ✓ Never click links in unexpected emails — go directly
  • ✓ Check the sender's actual email address carefully
  • ✓ Use SafeNet to scan any link before clicking
  • ✓ Enable two-factor authentication on important accounts
  • ✓ Report phishing emails to your email provider

info Real NZ Example

A common NZ phishing scam involves a fake NZ Post email claiming your parcel is on hold. The link looks like http://nzpost.delivery-confirm.ru/track — not the real nzpost.co.nz. Clicking it leads to a page that steals your credit card details.

What is Malware?

Malicious software designed to damage, spy on, or control your device.

deployed_code_alert

Malware includes viruses, spyware, ransomware, and trojans. It arrives through email attachments, fake software downloads, infected USB drives, or malicious websites. Once installed, it can steal data, encrypt files, or use your device to attack others.

Online Scams

Fraudulent schemes designed to trick you out of money or personal information.

money_off

Online scams come in many forms, often exploiting trust, urgency, or curiosity. Common scams in New Zealand include fake prize notifications, investment fraud, romance scams, and impersonation of IRD or ACC.

🚨 Common Scam Types

  • Prize scams — "You've won! Click to claim."
  • Investment fraud — Fake crypto or trading platforms
  • Romance scams — Building fake relationships to request money
  • Tech support — "Your computer has a virus, call us now"
  • Gov impersonation — Fake IRD or ACC messages

✅ How to Spot a Scam

  • If it sounds too good to be true, it probably is
  • Legitimate organisations never ask for gift card payments
  • Real IRD/ACC will not threaten immediate arrest
  • Always verify by calling the organisation on their official number
  • Never transfer money under pressure — urgency is deliberate
